Research topics designed to endure and evolve

As artificial intelligence rapidly transforms scientific and industrial methods, research topics—whether directly or indirectly related to AI—must account for growing uncertainty. Tools, approaches and the boundaries of knowledge may change faster than the duration of a PhD.

EDF R&D therefore aims to develop doctoral projects that look beyond short-lived technology trends, focusing on fundamental scientific questions that remain sufficiently open to evolve as discoveries and methods advance.

This adaptability comes with an essential requirement: maintaining a strong connection to EDF’s industrial challenges and the long-term needs of its business activities. The goal is to combine scientific depth, freedom to explore and lasting industrial relevance.

From internship to CIFRE PhD: plan ahead

Completing a research internship with an EDF R&D team is a particularly valuable route towards a CIFRE PhD. Many PhD projects build on an internship, giving both the candidate and the research team an opportunity to assess the scientific topic and their working relationship. An internship is not a formal prerequisite or a guarantee of a PhD offer.

Recruitment takes place throughout the year. Internship vacancies for a start around March are often published as early as October–November of the preceding year. Look for postings that explicitly mention a possible continuation into a PhD. Given scientific, contractual and administrative procedures, PhD projects frequently start between October and January–February, depending on the project.

Understanding the CIFRE scheme: the French National Association for Research and Technology (ANRT) ↗ administers CIFRE agreements on behalf of the French ministry responsible for research. The scheme brings together a company employing a doctoral researcher, an academic laboratory supervising the research and public funding. The ANRT website explains eligibility, procedures and official CIFRE guidance.

2. Explore scientific disciplines and societies

Scientific societies announce events and may advertise internships, PhDs and postdoctoral positions. Links lead to their main websites, not necessarily to vacancy boards.

Tip: subscribe to newsletters and mailing lists from scientific societies, CNRS research groups (GdR) and thematic networks relevant to your interests. They often circulate internship, PhD and postdoctoral opportunities, seminars and calls for projects. Also check their news, jobs and noticeboard sections.

EDF R&D: an international research organisation

Like the EDF Group itself, EDF R&D operates internationally. In addition to its French research sites, it has research operations in Germany (EIFER), Italy (Edison), the United Kingdom, China, Singapore and the United States. These teams conduct scientific programmes and collaborate with local academic and industrial partners.

To identify research teams, potential topics and internship, PhD or postdoctoral opportunities, explore EDF’s international research centre pages as well as local recruitment channels and official job listings. Application procedures and funding arrangements may differ from those in France.

4. Conferences and scientific exchanges

Conferences help you assess whether your interests match the research conducted at EDF R&D before applying. Explore scientific society meetings, thematic schools and seminar programmes. Identify presentations and posters authored by EDF R&D researchers, read their abstracts and, whenever possible, their papers.

Prepare two or three specific questions about methods, findings or unresolved challenges. A discussion after a session is often more productive than a generic internship request. You may then send a concise follow-up referring to the conversation, your skills and availability. Scientific contacts do not replace official application procedures.

5. Preparing a research application

A strong application goes beyond listing courses and software. Explain what you could contribute to the team: theoretical foundations, modelling, experiments, programming, simulation, data analysis, scientific computing, reproducibility or domain expertise.

Use a concrete project to demonstrate your skills: the research question, methods, your personal contribution, findings and limitations. If you use AI tools, explain what they helped you do, how you validated their outputs and what measurable value they added. Your ability to learn, read challenging papers, formulate hypotheses and work independently matters as much as proficiency with any particular tool.

For internships that may lead to a CIFRE PhD, explain your interest in a multi-year research project without assuming that doctoral funding has been secured.

6. Scientific research and artificial intelligence

AI-assisted research is already a reality. It supports different activities: office and document tasks, literature exploration, coding, data analysis, simulation, experimental design and hypothesis development. EDF is developing AI adoption strategies for office and scientific work, within access and security frameworks appropriate to each use case.

For an aspiring researcher, the decisive skill is not the ability to obtain a quick answer from an assistant; it is the ability to think independently about a new scientific problem. This means reading foundational and recent papers, comparing approaches, identifying hidden assumptions, testing findings and acknowledging uncertainty.

A thorough literature review is the starting point for serious research. It then feeds individual and collective reasoning at the whiteboard: reframing a question, building a proof, designing an experiment and resolving contradictions. AI can accelerate this process; it cannot replace reading, scientific judgement or creativity. All tools and data must be handled according to applicable confidentiality, intellectual property and security rules.
